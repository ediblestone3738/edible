While strolling down the grand, tree-lined avenue of **Andrássy út** in central Budapest, many visitors are surprised to come across an authentic slab of concrete that once divided East and West Berlin[cite: 1]. 

Situated directly outside the **House of Terror Museum** (Terror Háza Múzeum)[cite: 1], this striking memorial serves as a physical reminder of the Iron Curtain[cite: 1] and the shared Cold War history between Hungary and Germany.

---

## The Historical Connection: Hungary and the Fall of the Wall

Hungary played a pivotal role in the collapse of the Berlin Wall in 1989. In May 1989, Hungary began dismantling its electrified border fence with Austria. By August and September 1989, during the famous **Pan-European Picnic**, thousands of East German citizens fled to Western Europe through Hungary.

To honor Hungary's crucial role in breaking the Iron Curtain, original segments of the Berlin Wall were gifted and installed across Budapest as symbols of freedom and European unity[cite: 1].

## Visiting the House of Terror Museum

The Berlin Wall monument sits right outside the **House of Terror Museum** (Andrássy út 60)[cite: 1]. This location is profoundly symbolic:

* **Fascist & Communist Headquarters:** The building formerly served as headquarters for both the Arrow Cross Party (Hungarian Nazis) and the Communist secret police (AVH).
* **Museum Focus:** Opened in 2002, the museum honors victims of twentieth-century totalitarian regimes in Hungary[cite: 1].
* **Exterior Architecture:** The building is recognizable by its bold, overhanging roofline with cutouts spelling "TERROR" that cast dynamic shadows across the facade in sunlight.

* **Metro:** Take the historic **M1 (Yellow Line)** to **Vörösmarty utca** station[cite: 1]. The museum and monument are just a short 1-minute walk away.
* **Address:** Andrássy út 60, 1062 Budapest, Hungary.
